Pixel shift on the original Nikon Z7, right in the menu*

The first Z7 never got pixel shift. It also never got a shutter sound you can hear in silent mode, or upside-down photos that play back the right way up. This project adds those, plus custom grids, a battery percentage and a few other things. It all comes from small modules that load off the SD card and lean on Nikon's own firmware underneath.

How the pixel shift works

The Z7's in-body stabilisation can already move the sensor around. Nikon just never used it for this. The module borrows the stabiliser, nudges the sensor exactly one pixel at a time, and fires the shutter itself at each spot. After the group, a script on my computer combines the raw files into one image where every pixel has real red, green and blue data, instead of the camera guessing the missing colours.

It now lives in Nikon's own menu: MENU → Photo shooting → Pixel shift shooting. You pick 4, 8 or 16 shots, a delay, an interval, optional AE bracketing and a new folder per group. Then one full press does the rest. I tested every one of those on my camera over eight sessions, fixing what broke each time, and the raw files show the sensor landing where it should.

What a pixel-shift group looks like

  1. Camera on a tripod, mode dial on M, manual focus. Turn Pixel shift shooting on and pick your number of shots.
  2. Leave the menu, fully press the shutter once, and step back.
  3. It waits about 7 seconds for the camera to stop wobbling, then takes the group, one click per shot. It switches to RAW, single frame and silent mode for the group, then puts your settings back.
  4. It puts the sensor back where it was and hands stabilisation back to the camera. Press OK any time to stop early.
  5. Back home, run merge_z7_pixel_shift.py on the NEFs.

On a printed page, the merged image shows the halftone dots cleanly, where a normal shot turns them into coloured speckle. Smooth areas come out noticeably cleaner too. Getting here took a lot of wrong turns, like the camera still settling when the first shot fired, and every one of them is written up in PIXEL_SHIFT_RESEARCH.md.

*The fine print. It's hardware verified on my one camera, on a tripod, pointed at something that doesn't move. Some honest limits:
  • The menu row's name shows up blank on the camera right now. The option works; I'm still chasing the label.
  • 32 shots uses the same code as 16, but I haven't run it on the camera yet.
  • The merge happens on a computer, not in the camera.
  • It's still experimental, source-only firmware research. Read the risk notice first.

FAQ

Can the original Nikon Z7 do pixel shift?

Not from Nikon, no. With this, yes. It's in the Photo shooting menu: one full press takes 4, 8 or 16 shots moved by the camera's own stabilisation, and a computer merges them. It's hardware verified on my Z7, but still experimental.

Can the Z7 play a shutter sound in silent mode?

Yes, with the LCD module. It plays a WAV from the card every time you take a photo. You pick the volume and the sound.

Why does my Z7 show upside-down photos the wrong way up?

The stock firmware saves them as if they were the right way up, and playback can't rotate 180°. The module fixes both.

Will it work on a Z7 II?

No, this is for the original Z7 only. For the Z7 II, check out JKWTCN's z7ii-extensions. That's the project I based this on.

Is there a firmware file I can download?

No. It's source only. I don't share Nikon's firmware or any compiled modules. Please read the risk notice before you try building anything.
